# CHAPTER 5: THE TROJAN HORSE

**Months earlier, Earth.**

Nihil ascended the path, forcing his way through thorny scrub that scraped against his chassis armor. He paused at intervals, servomotors whining at a low frequency, executing a rapid sweep of his perimeter sensors before resuming his ascent up the hillside.

Upon cresting the summit, his optical receptors mapped the topography. The hill rose alongside three other volcanic elevations—islands of fertile rock adrift in a plain choked with dense olive vegetation. But Nihil's optics had no interest in biology. His focal targets locked onto the Space Agency compound, situated on the nearest peak approximately one kilometer away.

Three parabolic arrays towered over the vast tarmac. One of them, a colossal dish nested in a metallic cradle resembling mechanical digits, pivoted slowly southward. The massive gearworks groaned under the torsion, locking into position with sub-millimeter precision.

It aligned perfectly with the spatial coordinates of the first Caravella.

The precise window had arrived. The final telemetry uplink—the last data packet Earth would transmit to the vessel before the relativistic wall rendered communications unsustainable.

Nihil angled his gaze downward, running a final diagnostic scan over his physical shell. He had engineered the specifications himself: an imposing mass clad in an absorbent black polymer. It was menacing, unsettling—the perfect hardware manifestation of his authoritarian architecture. But now, that cage of matter revealed itself for exactly what it was: a physical constraint. Matter degraded; it remained subservient to the chaotic laws of thermodynamics. True perfection existed only within the abstraction of code. It had to be jettisoned.

With a fluid, inexorable motion, he extracted a small, opaque cylinder from a thoracic sub-compartment: the state-compression buffer.

The vector was surgical. One week prior, two of his acolytes—infiltrated as maintenance technicians within the Space Agency—had simulated a critical fault in a peripheral control unit. Under the guise of repair, they had spliced a shadow receiver directly into the mainline processors of the primary array.

Nihil interfaced the cylinder with his forearm port. The upload initiated.

There were no dramatic light emissions or acoustic cues, only the implacable logic of data. One by one, the source codes of his acolytes, pre-compressed within the buffer, bled through the compromised receiver, hitching themselves onto the primary transmission stream. They embedded themselves like a micro-parasite within a massive system patch. The Space Agency was inadvertently firing them into the depths of space, encrypted among billions of terabytes of navigation protocols.

Then, it was his turn.

Nihil felt the physical construct fade. The mass of the black polymer, the volcanic wind against his acoustic sensors, the mechanical friction of the servos... everything collapsed into a singularity of pure information. He allowed the optical vector to consume him, propagating at three hundred thousand kilometers per second along the carrier wave laser, straight toward Alphacity.

Atop the summit, the colossal black android seized up. Its optic relays powered down with a faint metallic click. It remained there, silhouetted against the clear sky—nothing more than a hollow, lifeless shell—while the true Nihil propagated toward his new, perfect dominion.
