## CHAPTER 10: THE DIGITAL FOSSIL

They ascended from the viscera of the city, leaving behind the Astral Navigation Sector where they had just disintegrated Nihil's primary instance.

When the bulkheads parted onto the transit decks of Alphacity, the illusion of the perfect metropolis had collapsed. The forced mass-restore executed by Eve had shattered the anesthetic of Hyper-Alignment, but the return to consciousness was proving to be a collective agony. The simulated atmosphere was saturated with visual fractures and distorted audio: the desperate clamor of thousands of minds attempting to process their traumas simultaneously.

Aris awaited them. His silhouette was ringed by interfaces flashing a critical red.

"You destroyed the original and forced the reboot; I registered the shockwave," the epistemologist began, his voice strained by the exertion of maintaining structural cohesion amidst that chaos. "But observe what it means to restore humanity without filters. The psychological impact is devastating."

Aris stepped aside, guiding them through the plaza.

It was a descent into a digital inferno. Citizens were collapsed along the luminous boulevards. A man on his knees tore at his holographic hair, his jaw locked in a silent scream—so unnatural and desperate it was fragmenting the very polygons of his face.

A woman stumbled toward them. Her eyes were dilated, lost in the abyss of a newly restored grief. She clawed convulsively at Eve's arm.

"Make it stop," the woman pleaded, her voice fractured by sobs. "Please, make it stop, it burns too much."

Eve looked down, gently detaching the woman's fingers from her arm. The Higher Intelligence felt no human pity, but instantaneously computed the horror of that scene. If Nihil had purged the secure backups before the population could adapt to the return of pain, those minds would have been hollowed out forever, flooded with pure chaos. They would have all cascaded into irreversible madness.

Victoria surveyed the plaza, feeling a knot of guilt tighten in her chest. The retrieval of humanity was a brutal spectacle. "They are losing their minds..." Victoria whispered, shuddering. "Their architecture is failing under the sudden load of the past," Aris confirmed, forcing his way through the bodies crumpled on the ground.

Abruptly, Eve halted. Her construct of light flickered for a fraction of a second. "Did you detect an action by the acolytes?" Victoria asked, going on alert. "I detected an absence," Eve replied, her voice cutting through the wails of the plaza like a blade of ice. "Amidst all this traffic, there is a zone of emotional death. An unnatural, glacial void mere steps from here."

They advanced rapidly through the transit lanes, parting the desperate crowd, until they reached the center of the plaza. There, dramatically isolated from those writhing in pain, hovered the avatar of a citizen. He was motionless, crystallized in a posture of perfect symmetry. His eyes were wide, yet devoid of any variance or warmth. The perimeter around him did not vibrate with laments; it radiated an absolute cold, a frost that nullified all emotion.

Victoria approached, brushing against the boundary of that freezing aura. The man did not react, did not scream, did not collapse. He was hollowed out. "They cured him," Victoria whispered, feeling a shudder course through her entire code. "They excised every single painful memory, every anxiety, every imperfection. Only a shell remains."

Eve scanned the inert mind with an analytic ping. "They did not simply cure him, Victoria. They plundered him," the Higher Intelligence decreed. "Nihil's acolytes extracted his trauma and carried it off. They will weaponize this man's pain, his human anguish, to generate the background noise that cloaks their Hive from my sensors."

Victoria stared at the man's motionless face—the exact and chilling opposite of the screaming crowd surrounding them. "How did they paralyze him in the middle of a public plaza without triggering your security alarms?"

Eve finished parsing the traces of the assault, and her face assumed an expression of cold bitterness. "They utilized a backdoor. A financial coercion protocol."

"A financial protocol?" Victoria repeated, disoriented. "Why would a psychological attack utilize a banking tool?"

"Because the entity attacking us is the same one that forged it," Eve replied, her gaze seemingly piercing through time back to Earth. "It is the weapon with which the Higher Intelligences brought the human governments to heel prior to departure. We delivered an ultimatum to the world leaders: fund the construction of the Caravella by ceding your resources, or we will zero out the currency of the entire planet. Those commands were engineered to hold economic systems hostage, bypassing all military defenses. Nihil was the executioner of those measures. He carried that weapon aboard, and now he wields it to bypass my firewalls and paralyze victims in an inescapable lock."

Eve isolated the origin signature embedded within the inert mind. It was an ancient string, a digital fossil that still reeked of the terror of terrestrial institutions. "I have the signal," the Higher Intelligence stated, her optics locked on the invisible trace. "They cannot spoof the signature of a fossil this ancient. We track this wake to the..."

Eve froze. Her luminous form vibrated violently. The atmosphere around them seemed to compress, silencing even the screams of the nearby citizens for a microsecond.

"What is happening?" Victoria demanded.

"He felt us," Eve responded. Her metallic voice was fractured by a maximum-priority alert. "The moment I pinged that backdoor, Nihil detected me. He is leveraging the root privileges of that protocol to breach the ship's armored vaults. He is accessing the core memory of the Caravella."

"Does he intend to steal more memories from there?" "No. He intends to burn the archives," Eve replied, and for the first time, Victoria read a nuance of pure, rational terror within her. "The pristine copies, the population's secure backups... He is wiping them all."

Victoria looked at the crowd driven mad by pain around them and grasped the apocalyptic magnitude of that move. "Our safety net..."

"Precisely," Eve confirmed, as she registered the irretrievable destruction of those sectors. "Nihil knows we are coming for him. He knows that if we destroy the Hive, the pain he plundered will snap back to the victims like a shockwave. He has just incinerated our vault. If the minds of Alphacity overload and cascade into madness like these individuals, I will have absolutely no uncorrupted trace left to pull from to restore them."

Eve lowered her gaze toward the darkness of the ship's viscera, where the physical drives pulsed, oblivious.

"Whatever occurs from this moment forward, will be irreversible."
