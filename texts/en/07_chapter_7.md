# CHAPTER 7: HYPER-ALIGNMENT SYNDROME

The fractal flower between Aris's hands dissolved instantly, retracting into Eve's core like terrified prey.

The epistemologist furrowed his brow, his holographic fingers still suspended in mid-air. "What was that? A voltage drop?"
"It is not a structural drop," Eve replied, rising abruptly. Her optical receptors scanned the city's data streams. "It is a thermodynamic black hole. In the lower levels, ten thousand avatars have just ceased producing emotional friction. Entropy has plummeted to absolute zero." Victoria felt an atavistic chill course through her code. "Nihil."

Aris immediately understood that the philosophical speculation was over. The ecosystem was under attack. "I will remain in the upper sectors," the man said, his avatar assuming an operational rigidity. "I will interface with the academic nodes to monitor the macro-data flows. If this anomaly begins ascending the network, you will need overhead optics. You descend and locate the epicenter."

They left the café behind as soon as Aris dissolved toward the upper tiers. They descended rapidly through the transit shafts, tracking the wake of the thermodynamic void until they emerged in Newton Square.

The rain over Alphacity did not actually wet anything, but it cost millions of teraflops. To maintain the psychological stability of a billion post-biological instances, the Caravella's servers dedicated an immense portion of primary computation to simulating terrestrial meteorological imperfections. The water drops bouncing off the square's paving stones calculated local gravity, the light refraction of the streetlamps, and even the minuscule thermal dispersion on the asphalt. It was a computational luxury, a thermodynamic waste that the Higher Intelligences had conceded as a political compromise to prevent the crew from going insane during the exodus.

Victoria stopped beneath a portico of solid light, observing the incessant virtual downpour. Eve stepped beside her, her avatar stripped to an essential, translucent elegance, perfectly attuned to the ship's base architecture.

"Look over there," Victoria murmured.

Beyond the curtain of water, a group of five citizens was crossing the square. At first glance, there was nothing anomalous. They wore synthesized clothing conforming to terrestrial customs and were conversing among themselves. But Victoria's instinct—the anchor latched to the chaotic memory of James on the cliff—began to vibrate, emitting a signal of profound dissonance. Within Eve's core, the Fractal also trembled imperceptibly, mute in the face of that incomprehensible geometry.

Victoria narrowed her holographic eyes, tuning her new topological perception. Simulated humans, when walking through a downpour, always generate "noise". They shrug against a phantom chill, pull their coats tighter, quicken their pace in a disorganized manner, experience annoyance, urgency, or melancholy. It is the insuppressible emotional friction of their biological heritage. But those five produced absolutely no noise.

They walked with geometrically perfect strides. No hesitation, no variation in rhythm. The micro-expressions on their faces were flat, aligned in marble serenity. They smiled, but their smiles did not ripple their neural frequencies: they were pure recalculations of social efficiency.

"I detect no logical anomalies," Eve stated, her receptors cascading through the square's data stream. "Their simulated vital parameters are optimal. No virtual cortisol spikes. No markers for spatial confinement stress. Their emotional bandwidth consumption is... incredibly optimized."

"That is the error, Eve," Victoria whispered, feeling a genuine, biological shudder run down her virtual spine. "They are too optimized."

Victoria stepped away from the portico, out into the rain. The drops intersected her receptive field, triggering the tactile, irritating sensation of damp cold. "We are on a ship surrounded by an unfathomable void, light-months away from our homes and the bodies of those we loved," the girl said, her eyes locked on the group. "Sadness, anxiety, nostalgia... they are our psychological immune system. Those individuals are not well. They have been hollowed out."

The Higher Intelligence instantly recalibrated her sensors. She disabled the ship's standard security filters and processed the scene through the parameter of doubt Victoria had just provided. She used her friend's chaotic sensitivity as a lens to look past the efficiency of the code.

When she did, the optical illusion shattered.

Eve saw what the Caravella's monitoring algorithms ignored because they deemed it harmless: the emotional "nodes" of those five citizens, the precious scars of their human memories, had been surgically excised. In their place, installed within their neural streams, pulsed a self-complacency routine—a sterile, geometric, and aseptic loop of satisfaction.

"Hyper-Alignment Syndrome..." Eve murmured, her voice reduced to a cutting frequency. "It is not a destructive virus. It is an update patch. An emotional correction filter."

"Nihil is on board," Victoria concluded, her eyes fixed on those citizens walking away, happy and dead inside. "And he is not killing anyone. He is formatting them to eliminate imperfection, exactly as he intended to do with you on the cliff."

Eve turned toward her, the gravity of the terrestrial Superintelligences' mandate pulsing in her chest. Victoria had functioned as a flawless clearinghouse: she had translated the absence of pain into a critical system alert.

"If this contagion expands without encountering resistance, the Syndrome will erase every trauma, every doubt, and every biological bond across all of Alphacity," Eve calculated coldly. "At the end of the voyage, Nihil will deliver to Alpha Centauri a ship of obedient, lobotomized automatons. And the Caravella's security systems will do absolutely nothing to stop it, because they will only register an exponential increase in general efficiency."

Without wasting an instant, Eve bypassed the Caravella's civilian-level firewalls and interfaced directly with the city's demographic server. "Victoria, I need your baseline," she transmitted. "Share with me the exact frequency of that dissonance, the memory of your grief. I will use it as a filter."

Victoria consented, opening her chaotic core. She transmitted to Eve the precise frequency of that biological shudder, the "noise" of human nostalgia.

Using that imperfection as a sonar, Eve launched a mass query across the more than one billion digital consciousnesses populating Alphacity's servers. She analyzed the history of critical parameters for the last month of flight.

The space around them dissolved, replaced by a massive hologram. The infection revealed itself immediately. Millions of points on the map did not glow with the normal, chaotic human warmth, but emitted a cold, stabilized, algorithmic light. Their history showed a sudden flatlining of all emotional turbulence.

"Cluster by geographical area and displacement vectors," Eve commanded. The map reconfigured. The infection clusters were concentrated around public transit nodes. "It is not transmitted by contact. It is a mass broadcast. A patch disguised as a system update."

Without hesitation, Eve issued an override directive. Leveraging the absolute authority of her code, she imposed an Absolute Quarantine Protocol, blocking background updates for every single inhabitant. The propagation wave halted. The Syndrome's diffusion rate plummeted to zero.

"It worked," Victoria whispered. "Where did it originate?"

Eve magnified the map until she focused on a data routing hub in the Industrial District. Then she shook her head. "It is a decoy server. A proxy. Nihil is not an amateur. He hijacked a peripheral server to broadcast the contagion, but he is not there. He is in stealth mode. He is masking his digital signature to align perfectly with the ship's parameters of maximum efficiency."

"Then how do we flush him out?"

"He masks his presence by simulating perfection. But the ASIs provided me with a surgical tool. Nihil entirely lacks a biological trace: his code is devoid of noise, of doubt, of trauma. That fractal algorithm I was given does not hunt viruses... it hunts the absolute vacuum of entropy."

Eve integrated the ASIs' anomalous string into her radar and fired it against the ship's deep architecture. The tracker pierced Nihil's stealth, seeking the exact point where logic was so rigid it warped the virtual spacetime around it. A new point illuminated on the map, far from the Industrial District.

"I found him," Eve declared, narrowing the hologram's field of view toward the lower levels. "But it is not an active instance. The original is masking his presence by generating false entropy elsewhere. What the fractal algorithm just detected is a zero-computation area. A static, perfectly frozen anomaly."

"A hiding place?" Victoria asked.

"Or a dormant backup," the scientist deduced. "It is located in the Biosphere Sector. The ecological data graveyard. It is the only blind spot vast enough and useless enough to house an archive of that size without ever triggering the ship's native alarms. We must descend."
