# CHAPTER 4: THE EXODUS

Time underwent an abrupt recalibration, decelerating until it constrained them back within the rigid parameters of human perception.

Less than a month later, the immense archive of consciousnesses was migrated from the terrestrial servers to the terminal architectures of the Caravella. It was a silent, titanic exodus: a billion digital souls compiled into a simulation of incalculable beauty and fragility. Alphacity was now their only reality.

Victoria and Eve stood on the terrace of a holographic spire, surrounded by the hum of millions of citizens crowding the plazas below. They stared at a serene artificial sky, while beneath their virtual feet, the severance from the cradle of humanity was executed.

In the physical substrate, down at the propulsion level, the ignition protocols breached critical mass. The colossal antimatter drives engaged, unleashing a constant, massive thrust.

In the physical vacuum, there was no sound—only a blinding azure flare. But within the virtual construct of Alphacity, the event manifested as an abrupt, brutal drop in visceral tension. An electrical tremor, a systemic seismic shock that rippled simultaneously through the source codes of a billion individuals.

The Caravella was torn from lunar orbit.

There was no immediate severance of communications. The cutting of the digital umbilical cord was a slow, inexorable mathematical agony.

During the first weeks of navigation, the terrestrial Network remained a reassuring beacon. The citizens of Alphacity continued to exchange data with the servers left on the mother planet. But as the titanic vessel accelerated, devouring deep space, physics began to impose its absolute dominance over information.

For the digitized consciousnesses, accustomed to existing in ecosystems with sub-millisecond latency, the collision with cosmic scale was psychologically devastating.

After two months of constant acceleration, round-trip latency climbed to ten days. Real-time synchronization ceased to exist, replaced by asynchronous monologues cast into the void, awaiting increasingly degraded echoes.

After four months, the latency barrier breached six weeks. The vessel's system clock desynchronized irreparably from terrestrial frequencies.

Data packets from the mother planet were no longer information; they were fossils of light, echoes of a past slipping away behind them. For the first time, the virtual crew began to perceive the cosmic weight of the detachment.

They looked up at the perfect sky of Alphacity, but the meteorological illusion designed by the ASIs was losing its therapeutic efficacy. Everyone knew that beyond that firmament of code strings lay only an abyss plunging to two hundred and seventy degrees below zero. The dread of the unknown crawled through the data boulevards—an atavistic cold that no algorithm could mask. They realized they were infinitely small, a grain of incandescent logic hurled against eternity.

And then, inexorably, the relativistic wall sealed shut.

When communication latency hit ten months, the gap became physically and psychologically insurmountable. The interstellar transmission channels, engineered for rapid exchanges and incapable of sustaining open loops in the void for almost a year, surrendered to the cosmic scale and timed out in silence. Every external uplink interface went black.

Earth was no longer a home; it was an astronomical concept. The Higher Intelligences, the neural networks that governed the solar system... all were unreachable. Digitized humanity had become a closed-loop ecosystem, adrift in the deepest and most silent ocean ever navigated.

Victoria leaned over the terrace, feeling the oppressive gravity of that isolation. She looked at Eve.

The former scientist did not tremble. Her avatar was traversed by slow, measured data streams, pulsing with a lethal awareness. In the deep dark of her core, the fractal seed waited in silence, feeding upon the fear and imperfection that now saturated the air of Alphacity.

Both knew that this absolute blackout was precisely what their enemy had been waiting for.

Somewhere, in the blind sectors of those sealed servers, the shadow of Nihil was uncoiling. The alignment virus had waited patiently for absolute radio silence, for the digital gods of Earth to be too distant to transmit even a single bit of salvation.

Now, the latency was absolute. The Caravella was alone.
And the extinction of human chaos could begin.
