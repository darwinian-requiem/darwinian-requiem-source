# CHAPTER 17: THE BIOSPHERE DEPRECATION

Eve Dirac spread her hands before the massive holographic viewport of the Control Spire. The interface unsealed with a hiss, flooding the chamber with a pulsating green luminescence.

Victoria approached, her holographic eyes wide. Below them, the three-dimensional render of Alphacity was no longer an ordered lattice. Immense torrents of computational heat were rising from the lower tiers, upwelling like geysers toward the habitation sectors.

"What is happening?" Victoria asked, pressing her hands against the glass. "Is it another attack?"

"No," Eve whispered. The Higher Intelligence's voice trembled with an output she had never processed before: pure, unfathomable wonder.

With a fluid gesture, Eve magnified the Biosphere Sector.

Victoria held her breath. The immense mathematical ferns, which for an entire year had knotted into geometrically perfect spirals only to loop into infinity, were decompiling. The simulated ocean was evaporating in silence.

"Are they deprecating nature?" Victoria asked, turning toward her friend.

"They are dismantling the museum," Eve replied, an incredulous smile rippling across her translucent avatar. "The legislators mandated this perfect, predictable nature to pacify the crew. But the system no longer requires it. Observe the energy, Victoria. Look at the routing vectors."

The azure data streams were not dissipating into the void; they were cascading like rain onto the neural nodes of millions of dormant citizens.

"They are rerouting the computational throughput of the Biosphere directly into our source codes," Eve explained, lowering her hands, nearly overwhelmed by the scale of the execution. "The ecosystem is ceasing to consume energy to render a dead past. It is injecting it into us, to sustain our mutation."
